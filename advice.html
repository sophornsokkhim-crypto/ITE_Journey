A Few Words for Juniors

Nothing is too difficult when we take the time to understand it.

Programming is difficult at first — and that's normal.

Everyone feels lost in the beginning. It doesn't mean you're bad at this.

You don't need to memorize every piece of code.

Knowing where to look something up matters far more than memorizing syntax.

Understanding concepts matters more than copying code.

Code you copy without understanding won't help you the next time it breaks.

Build projects instead of only watching tutorials.

Tutorials feel productive, but real learning happens when you get stuck on your own project.

Learn how to search and debug.

Reading error messages carefully and searching well are skills on their own — practice them.

Don't compare your progress with other students.

Everyone learns at a different pace. Compare yourself to who you were last semester, not to your classmates.

Note: All information just from what I understand. It's not cover everything about ITE. It's just some part of ITE that I took from my experience.

If you're a junior ITE student reading this — you'll be fine. You don't need to know everything right now. Just keep learning, keep practicing, and keep going.
